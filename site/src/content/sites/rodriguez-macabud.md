## What the images show

The 1988–1990 composite shows the Macabud hills under grass, brush and forest, with no quarry pits. By
2016–2017 several pits had already opened in the south and east of the box. By 2026 the pits form a
chain of pale benches and small pit lakes running north–south through the east half, with a broad bare
area at the southern edge. ATN's rectangle is in the west of the box. Its pit is new since 2016–2017 and
shows as a separate patch of magenta west of the main chain.

The magenta layer counts about 156 ha that went from green to bare between 1988–90 and 2026, and about
137 ha between 2016–17 and 2026. Much of the quarry ground was grass rather than dense green before, so
these figures undercount the area dug. The images do not show which permit each eastern pit belongs to.

## Who operates here

MGB's September 2025 directory lists ATN Holdings under MPSA No. 354-2024-IVA, an 82.7092-hectare
commercial basalt and aggregate agreement in Rodriguez. That figure is the authorized agreement area,
not a measurement of bare or cleared ground.

ATN's environmental impact statement supplies an unusually precise location: a rectangle from
14°47′00″ to 14°47′30″ N and 121°08′00″ to 121°08′30″ E. It covers the western quarry in this view.
Several other pits are visible farther east in Macabud, around 121.15°–121.17° E, but none of the opened
official records ties those pits to ATN or another permit holder, and the images alone cannot establish
an operator or whether work is authorized.

## What the company and government say

ATN told the Philippine Stock Exchange in July 2018 that quarry operations had begun and that its crusher
was ready for commercial operation after testing. This is the earliest sourced evidence of physical
quarrying found for the Macabud project. The company later moved through an exploration permit, a Special
Mines Permit and finally MPSA No. 354-2024-IVA, issued in January 2024.

MGB temporarily suspended ATN's mineral-processing permit in December 2020 while a DENR composite team
assessed whether quarrying and crushing operations contributed to the Typhoon Ulysses flooding. When MGB
announced the lifting in February 2021, it said quarrying's contribution to flooding and siltation was
minimal compared with exceptional rainfall, prior watershed saturation, rapid urbanisation, erosion,
deforestation and other disturbance. Lifting was subject to drainage, settling and desilting conditions
where required.
