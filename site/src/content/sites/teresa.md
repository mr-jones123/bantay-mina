## What the images show

There is no "before quarrying" image. Both main quarry areas were already open in the 1988–1990
composite, as pale patches in the north and the south-west of the box, among farms and brush. By 2026 the
northern area is a long white quarry running north–south, and the southern one, where Republic Cement's
pits are, has deepened into white benches beside a dark pit lake. Smaller pits with pit lakes have
opened along the top edge of the box, and clusters of white rectangular structures have appeared in the
east since 2016–2018. The images do not show what those structures are.

The magenta layer counts about 365 ha that went from green to bare between 1988–90 and 2026, and about
575 ha between 2016–18 and 2026. Most of it is not quarry ground: it is housing spreading out from
Antipolo and Teresa in the west, and farm plots that were green in one dry season and bare in the other.
The quarries themselves were mostly bare already in 2016–2018, so they add little to the second figure.

The north-east corner of the box just reaches the Upper Marikina River Basin Protected Landscape as BMB
maps it. None of the cleared ground is inside it. The images do not show which permit each pit belongs to.

## Who operates here

This box cuts across several adjoining quarry agreements, and a white patch in a satellite image should not be
assigned from a company name or barangay alone. The most precise public records reviewed are the corner schedules
in Republic Cement's environmental report, Teresa Marble's project description and the PH-EITI contract annex.
They support three qualified matches.

The southern pits beside the Teresa cement plant are Republic Cement's operation under MPSA No. 138-99-IV.
The agreement corners span approximately 14.5398–14.5642° N and 121.1981–121.2075° E, covering the observed
pits near 14.536–14.553° N, 121.200–121.207° E except for the very southern edge of the visual estimate. Republic's
report says all three existing quarries supplying the plant were within No. 138. Its separate MPSA No. 159-2000-IV
lies immediately west, at roughly 14.5485–14.5568° N and 121.1972–121.2014° E, but the same report said in 2021
that No. 159 had not yet been converted to commercial extraction. The visible Republic pits therefore should not
be attributed to No. 159 without later approval evidence.

The long central quarry is not one cleanly documented polygon. Teresa Marble's MPSA No. 125 Amended A runs
approximately 14.5712–14.5781° N and 121.2029–121.2076° E. Its ECC production block is inside that area. Farther
north, the precisely published portions of MPSA No. 124 extend from about 14.5784 to 14.5881° N and 121.2103 to
121.2172° E. Teresa Marble operates a 10.9011-hectare portion; its 2023 description says Island Quarry operates the
other 46.5019 hectares. Those coordinates match the middle and northern portions of the pale corridor, but do not
account for every part of the visual estimate from 14.555 to 14.593° N. The apparent plant near 14.59° N and the
gaps between the published polygons are left unassigned rather than inferred from proximity.

The smaller north-eastern pits intersect two documented agreement polygons. Island Quarry's 70.98-hectare MPSA
No. 116-98-IVA spans about 14.5962–14.6139° N and 121.2144–121.2268° E. Immediately east, Rapid City's
54.0216-hectare MPSA No. 118-98-IVA spans about 14.5917–14.6083° N and 121.2258–121.2323° E. Together those
survey coordinates match the western and central part of the observed cluster around 14.595–14.605° N. Clearings
east of about 121.2323° E, including those approaching the box's eastern edge, are not identified by the official
polygons reviewed here.

All hectare figures above are legal agreement or ECC areas. They are not estimates of how much land has been
cleared. The records also do not establish the first year of surface disturbance at any of the three clusters. The
1998–2000 agreement and ECC dates show when particular instruments were issued, not when quarry benches first
opened.

## What residents say

Teresa Marble's project description reports an October 2022 survey of 87 residents and institutional respondents
in San Jose. Respondents described blasting that they believed had cracked floors and walls and damaged appliances,
and they reported muddy roads during rain. They also feared that blasting or tree cutting could contribute to
landslides or flooding and that the proposed expansion could increase dust and air pollution. These statements are
community reports and anticipated effects recorded by the proponent's consultant; the document does not establish
that the quarry caused each reported condition.

The same survey provides important context: 65 respondents, or 74.71%, said the proposed project would be a big
help to the community, while smaller groups said it would help somewhat, would not help, or would be harmful.
MGB's August 2022 account separately identifies San Jose as Island Quarry's host community and May-Iba in Teresa
as a neighbouring community for the social-development program covering MPSA Nos. 116 and 124.

## What the permits and project documents say

Republic Cement's January 2000 ECC set ceilings of 2 million tonnes of limestone and 600,000 tonnes of diorite a
year. Its 2021 environmental report proposed increasing limestone output to 4.31 million tonnes a year to supply a
new integrated cement plant. That report is an EIA application, not evidence that DENR approved the higher limit.
It also describes drilling and controlled blasting in Quarries 1 and 3, much less blasting in softer Quarry 2, vibration
monitoring, and dewatering because Quarry 1 is mostly flooded during wet months.

Teresa Marble's February 2010 ECC covered 18.6 hectares and 48,000 tonnes a year of marble blocks. Its 2023
scoping document proposed extending the covered project area and producing 2 million tonnes of boulders plus
100,000 tonnes of marble blocks a year. The document identifies dust as the principal impact of the modification
and proposes road sprinkling, silt traps and settling ponds; retained water would be reused for sprinkling. These are
predictions and proposed controls, not findings that dust, runoff or flooding has been eliminated.

EMB's national ECC registry records a separate August 2018 certificate for Island Quarry's Pamias 1 and Pamias 3
limestone-quarry project in San Jose and San Luis. The registry does not state which MPSA polygon contains those
parcels, so it is not used here to label a particular pit.

## The 2018 suspension and resumption

The September 2018 stoppage was a temporary regional safety review after the fatal landslides in Itogon and Naga,
not a site-specific finding against the Teresa–Antipolo operators. MGB says DENR lifted most assessed quarry
suspensions on September 27. Its list of operations that had resumed by October 9 included Republic Cement's
MPSA No. 138, Teresa Marble/Island Quarry's No. 124, Island Quarry's No. 116 and Rapid City's No. 118. The source
does not give a separate lifting date for each permit.

## Limits of the public record

The records reviewed do not establish when surface quarrying first began at the Republic, Teresa Marble/Island
Quarry or north-eastern Island Quarry/Rapid City sites. They also do not support assigning the entire long central
scar to one holder, identifying the easternmost small pits, or treating a permit's hectares as cleared hectares. No
quarry-specific court judgment about dust, blasting, water or flooding was located in the reviewed official sources;
those subjects are presented as residents' reports or environmental-assessment issues, not adjudicated facts.
