## What the images show

In the 2006–2009 composite the valley is forested, with the river winding through it. Small-scale workings
already existed then, but none are large enough to show up at 30 m. By 2026 a pale, bare scar about 3 km
long runs down the middle of the box. Switchback roads climb the slope to its east, and a tan-coloured pond
fills a side valley to the southwest. The magenta layer shows every pixel that went from green vegetation to
bare ground or water.

The Sentinel-2 pair starts during commissioning, when clearing was already under way. Most of the change
since then is the scar spreading north and the southwestern pond filling.

## Who lives here

The Supreme Court's 2021 decision records that residents who challenged the mine in 2012 identified
themselves as members of Ifugao, Kalanguya and Cordillera Indigenous Cultural Communities. Their claims
included that open-pit mining threatened ancestral land. The decision describes these as the residents'
allegations, not findings by the Court.

FCF's 2020–2021 report names Kankanaey, Ibaloi, Kalanguya, Tuwali and Ayangan participants in a Runruno
Indigenous Peoples Organization weaving project set up with the barangay government and the National
Commission on Indigenous Peoples. The same company report says the mine does not operate in or next to
Indigenous territories.

## What residents say

The documented barricade in the sources below happened in December 2012. Residents tried to stop an access
road from being cleared. The Philippine Daily Inquirer reported six injuries. Police said they were there to
keep order and did not take sides.

Relocation remained disputed in 2020. MGB Region II reported that FCF had settled 215 of 407 land claims,
while other occupants rejected the compensation offered. After the November landslides, DENR asked the local
government and FCF to count households and find safer relocation sites.

## What the government and company say

The 2015 MGB order was a partial suspension after typhoon damage, not an outcome of the 2016–2017 DENR mine
audit. Metals Exploration said Runruno was omitted from that audit because the project was still pre-operating
and was not among the mines ordered suspended or closed in 2017.

FCF is wholly owned by Metals Exploration plc. The parent company's 2025 annual report says all operating
permits remain in place. It expects mining to finish in the second half of 2026 and ore processing by the end
of that year.
