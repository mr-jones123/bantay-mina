## What the images show

Mining here stopped in 1984, so both images show a closed mine. The main thing they show is how little has
changed. The pale, bare ground of the old mine complex, north-west of the river bends, is almost the same
shape in 1988–1990 as in 2026, and a small pond still sits inside it. More than forty years after closure,
it has not grown back. A second bare patch to the south is also in both images.

The Sentinel-2 pair adds a new pale clearing near the top of the box, which appears after 2018. Three small
quarry permits exist in Barlo, but their locations are not published, so this site does not say whose it
is. Most of the scattered magenta in the Sentinel-2 pair is dry-season grassland and burn scars, not mining.

## Where it is

The mapped disturbance is the old Barlo mine complex in Barangay Barlo, Mabini, Pangasinan, close to the
Zambales border. Recent government and MGB-supplied accounts both place the abandoned mine in Mabini.
The large river beside the mine area is the Balincaguin River, which the provincial government identifies
as one of Mabini's principal natural features.

## What was mined

Acoje Mining Company operated Barlo as a copper–gold–zinc project from 1974 until October 1984. Its
2005 project history describes early tunnels and adits into high-grade copper outcrops, followed by a
1,000-tonne-per-day mill.

Pyro Copper later received MPSA No. 153-2000-1. The Supreme Court records that the agreement covered
pyrite ore and other deposits and that the DENR secretary cancelled it in 2005.

## What the government and companies say

MGB's 2024 directory lists three separate, producing quarry permits for siliceous material in
Barangay Barlo. Each is about five hectares. The directory gives no permit polygons, so it does not
establish which individual patches in the 7 × 7 kilometre image belong to which holder, or whether
all three fall inside the image.

In 2023 the provincial board endorsed Northern Arc Minerals Corporation's application for an
environmental management and rehabilitation plan at Barlo. That endorsement is not a production
permit and does not show that the proposed work was completed. In early 2024, MGB and JOGMEC used the
abandoned site for training intended to identify sources of mine pollution and analyse mine drainage.
The public account provides no laboratory results, so it does not by itself establish the extent or
downstream effect of any contamination.
