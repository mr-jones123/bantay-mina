## What the images show

The first image predates the large mines. The Landsat archive has no scenes here before 1994, and few
after, so it combines every April–September view of 1994–2002. Those are the clearest months on Dinagat,
which has no real dry season. The box looks across the inlet at Malinao, which opens to the sea on the
right. In 1994–2002 the ridges on both sides of the inlet are forest and brush, with a few small brown
patches.

By 2025–26 there are two blocks of bare orange-brown ground: one on the ridge north of the inlet, and a
larger ring of benches and haul roads on the ridge to the south. The magenta layer counts about 220 ha
that went from green to bare, almost all of it inside the mapped mine footprint. The Landsat "after"
uses two seasons, 2025 and 2026, because one season left cloud gaps.

The Sentinel-2 pair shows smaller change after 2016–18, when both blocks were already open: the benches
have grown along their edges, and new clearing appears to the west. The page does not assign individual
patches to one of the three agreements that meet here.

## Who operates here

The centre of this box is in Malinao, Tubajon. PhilAtlas places the barangay at 10.2574° N,
125.6181° E, about 2.4 kilometres west of the box centre and inside its 9-kilometre width. Official
records place the Palhi and SR Metals mine sites in Malinao, where three adjoining agreement areas
meet. Norweah holds MPSA No. 242-2007-XIII and Oriental Vision operates it. East Coast Mineral
Resources holds MPSA No. 233-2007-XIII, with Libjo Mining and Westernshore as operators. Vista Buena
holds MPSA No. 031-94-X, operated since 2022 by SR Metals.

The agreements are much larger than the image box. They extend west into Libjo and north toward
Loreto; the Libjo Mining project also reaches Navarro and San Antonio, whose mapped centres lie west
of this view. The box is therefore a view of the mines above the Malinao watershed, not a map of the
whole 10,000-plus-hectare combined permit area.

## Why the mineral reservation matters

Proclamation No. 391 made Dinagat part of the Surigao Mineral Reservation in 1939. That declaration
reserved minerals to the state and closed the area to ordinary mining location and lease; it was not a
blanket permit for any present-day operator. The three numbered production-sharing agreements and
their environmental and operating approvals provide the later authority to mine.

Residents can hold tax declarations or longstanding customary claims inside a mineral reservation
without owning the minerals below. Climate Rights International reports that this split is central to
current disputes over the use of farms, stockpile areas and access routes. Those accounts are land-use
and compensation allegations, not court findings about title.

## What residents say

Malinao residents told Climate Rights International in 2025 that runoff from the ridge turns their
spring-fed water muddy after heavy rain. Farmers said sediment had left rice fields unusable, while
fisherfolk reported smaller catches and mud in crabs and mangroves. Some residents associated dry-season
dust with coughing and other illness. These are attributed accounts. The report did not publish water,
air or medical testing that could assign each claimed harm to one company, and three operations drain
toward the same community.

A separate peer-reviewed study based on interviews in May 2024 found a mixed picture. Participants
credited the mining company studied with employment, roads and some public services. Respondents near
the mining area were more likely to report worse spring water and raised concerns about dust,
deforestation, land degradation and noise. The authors measured perceptions, not pollutant levels or
medical causation.

## What local officials have said

Tubajon Mayor Fely Pedrablanca told Climate Rights International that mining threatens local
watersheds and water quality. The municipality's 2024 Watershed Code identifies more than 6,200
hectares in the Sambonotan, Navarro and Imelda watersheds for protection. In August 2026, Navarro
residents opposing a proposed additional project asked officials for wider consultation and disclosure
while the province was under a water-shortage state of calamity. That dispute concerns a proposal west
of this image box and does not establish the cause of the shortage.

## What the government and companies say

DENR's 2016 field validation found that Oriental Vision and Libjo Mining had impaired watershed
functions and caused coastal-water siltation. The Wellex audit also recorded failures involving
progressive rehabilitation, water permits, tree-cutting permits and environmental controls. DENR issued
cancellation orders in 2017, but later proceedings changed the Libjo and Wellex outcomes to conditional
suspensions. In 2022, DENR restored Vista Buena's agreement and MGB approved SR Metals as the new
operator. MGB's 2024 production table records output from all three permit areas.

Climate Rights International wrote to Oriental Vision, Libjo Mining, SR Metals and their respective
agreement holders before publishing its 2025 report. It reported that none had replied by publication.
The lack of a response is not proof of the allegations. The current MGB listing shows the projects as
permitted producers, while the older audit findings and current resident accounts remain part of the
public record.
