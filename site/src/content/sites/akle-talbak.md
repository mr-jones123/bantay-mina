## What the images show

The 1988–1990 Landsat composite comes from before the Eagle Cement project. It shows farmland, grass and
brush on low hills, crossed by a few tracks, with no plant and no quarry. By 2026 the cement plant sits in
the lower part of the box, next to a quarry with a blue-green pit lake. A second, larger white pit has been
opened north of it. The Sentinel-2 pair shows that this northern pit is new since 2016–2018, when only the
plant and the quarry beside it were there.

The magenta layer counts about 86 ha that went from green to bare between 1988–90 and 2026, and about
190 ha between 2016–18 and 2026. The second figure is larger because the 10 m images also pick up many
small farm plots in the west and north of the box that were green in one dry season and bare in the other.
Most of the quarry ground itself was not dense green before, so it counts only partly.

The box reaches north to the area of Eagle's northern parcels and Biak-na-Bato National Park. At 10 m no
large new bare ground shows there in 2026. The park's boundary is not drawn, because the official records
of it conflict (see below), and the images do not show which permit parcel each pit belongs to.

## On the Sierra Madre

Eagle Cement's 2022 environmental performance report, filed with the EMB, places the quarry area of
San Ildefonso and Doña Remedios Trinidad "in the vicinity of the western foothills of Southern Sierra
Madre Range".

## Who operates here

Eagle Cement operates the integrated cement plant at Akle and holds MPSA Nos. 181-2002-III and
245-2007-III. San Miguel Equity Investments, a wholly owned San Miguel Corporation subsidiary, acquired
99.9581% of Eagle in December 2022, but Eagle remains the named MPSA holder and plant operator.

MPSA 181 covers 169.3725 hectares. Eagle's 2022 environmental report identifies Parcels 14 and 15,
totalling 67.35 hectares, as its existing quarry areas and proposed increasing the approved quarry-project
area to 121.32 hectares by adding four parcels. MPSA 245 covers 82.6033 hectares, while its 2020 ECC
describes a smaller project footprint of about 69.9 hectares. These are agreement or approved project
areas, not measurements of bare ground or of land already quarried.

The plant's first three integrated production lines entered service between 2010 and 2018. The 2023 ECC
approved a proposed fourth line and additional quarry areas, but Eagle's 2025 annual report still describes
the Akle plant as having three integrated lines. The public record reviewed here therefore does not show
that Bulacan Line 4 has been completed or has begun operating.

## What residents say

Participants in the March and May 2022 community consultations asked how Eagle would control dust,
address the availability of water, manage blasting risks, provide local jobs and support livelihoods and
health programmes. At the September public hearing, an Akle participant also asked the company to
address drainage from the operation reaching a nearby river. These were concerns recorded during the
company's environmental-review process, not findings that permit limits or environmental standards had
been breached.

## What local officials have said

At the September 2022 public hearing, a Talbak barangay official asked whether the strength of quarry
blasts could be reduced. A San Ildefonso municipal representative raised dust from hauling, while the
municipal engineering office asked about damaged roads and truck-load controls. Eagle said it would
investigate unusually loud incidents, was considering more road sweepers, had road-rehabilitation
projects and would work with the LGU on road maintenance. Those statements are company responses,
not an independent assessment that the concerns were resolved.

## What the courts have decided

The reported Supreme Court case connected to Eagle's Akle mineral rights was a dispute among mining
companies, not a case brought by residents. In 2008 the Court upheld a 2001 memorandum under which
Eagle and Union Cement agreed to exchange overlapping mining claims and surface rights in San
Ildefonso. The ruling enforced that compromise; it made no finding about quarry pollution or community
harm.

## What the government and company say

The September 2018 stoppage was a short, region-wide precautionary action, not a site-specific finding of
wrongdoing. MGB ordered a 15-day suspension of quarrying in Central Luzon and seven other regions while
safety and geohazard assessments followed deadly landslides elsewhere. DENR allowed Eagle to resume
on September 27 after the review.

Eagle's environmental filings say it waters quarry roads, uses bag filters and siltation ponds, monitors
blast noise and vibration, and warns nearby communities before blasting. At the 2022 hearing the company
said it would locate and inspect the drainage point raised by an Akle participant and that barangay
representatives joined effluent monitoring. These are the company's descriptions of its controls. The 2025
annual report lists both MPSAs as commercially operating and says MPSA 245 began commercial limestone
operation in May 2024.

## Protected areas nearby

Proclamation No. 223 created Biak-na-Bato National Park in 1937 with an approximate area of 2,117
hectares. Proclamation No. 2204 excluded 330.3062 hectares for mineral development in 1982, and
Proclamation No. 84 revoked that action and returned the tract to the park in 1987. Proclamation No. 401
then redefined the boundary in 1989: it described two national-park lots totalling 658.8497 hectares and
separately set aside a 952.343-hectare mineral reservation, a 938.7805-hectare watershed forest reserve and
a 480.59-hectare forest reserve.

The official area records are not consistent. A 2013 BMB bulletin lists Biak-na-Bato as a 2,117-hectare
initial component of the National Integrated Protected Areas System and marks it “for disestablishment.”
BMB's current Protected Area Information System instead reports 3,030.56 hectares and lists both
Proclamations 223 and 401 as legal bases. Republic Act No. 7586 designated previously proclaimed national
parks as initial NIPAS components. Biak-na-Bato is not named in Republic Act No. 11038's legislated list of
protected areas; the amended law provides a separate congressional-establishment process for remaining
initial components. Subject to the rules for each protected-area category, Section 20 of the amended law
prohibits mineral exploration or extraction and commercial or large-scale quarrying within protected areas.
None of these texts establishes where an MPSA parcel lies relative to the controlling boundary.

Eagle's 2022 environmental report—not a government boundary determination—states that the park is
adjacent to Parcel 5 of MPSA 181. The filing classifies Parcels 5 and 6 as inactive, says Parcel 5 will not be
quarried, and identifies Parcel 6 as a proposed additional quarry area about 600 metres from the park. It
says Eagle was then quarrying only Parcels 15C and 15D. The 2023 ECC approved the wider Line 4 and
additional-quarry project, but approval alone does not establish that extraction later began in Parcel 6.
