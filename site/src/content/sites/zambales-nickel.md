## What the images show

This box is 15 km wide, larger than the others, to take in the whole district. In the 2002–2005 composite
the hills are a patchwork of forest, brush and dry brown grassland, with no large bare areas. By 2026
orange-brown laterite pits and stockpiles cover large parts of the north and centre of the box, and new
clearings reach into the south.

Much of this land was grass or brush in the dry season, not dense green vegetation, so the vegetation →
bare figure undercounts the area disturbed here. The mapped mine footprint gives a better sense of scale.
The Sentinel-2 pair starts during the 2016–2018 suspension years, and shows clearing that has spread since
then, mostly in the south.


## Who operates here

This is a mining district, not one mine. MGB records list four large nickel agreements across the box:
ZDMC's MPSA No. 191-2004-III, Eramen's No. 209-2005-III, BenguetCorp's No. 226-2005-III and
Filipinas Mining's No. 268-2008-III, operated by LNL. The Supreme Court's case record places their
operations in Guisguis, Guinabon, Lucapon South and Uacon, and across Santa Cruz and Candelaria.

## What residents say

Residents of Guisguis, Canaynayan, Guinabon and Lomboy told a 2012 fact-finding mission that red
sediment was filling rivers, creeks and irrigation channels. Farmers said floods had become faster and
more frequent and rice yields had fallen. Residents said fish and shrimp had become scarce. These are
residents' accounts. The 2014 government inspection separately examined complaints about flooded farms
and fishponds.

## What local officials have said

Local positions have not been uniform. In 2009, the Santa Cruz mayor told PCIJ that provincial mining
permits had left the town with little control. The governor said he opposed environmental damage but not
mining itself. In a separate 2012 dispute over LNL's Bolitoc port, the municipal council had endorsed the
project while the mayor ordered clearing stopped.

## What the government and companies say

The official record shows enforcement as well as later permission to resume. The EMB and MGB acted in
2014 over laterite siltation and mining methods. Conditions for temporary reopening included sediment
monitoring, river rehabilitation and payment of fishpond claims. In later court filings, BenguetCorp and
LNL denied violating environmental laws, while ZDMC said an assessment found that it did not contribute
to the reported siltation. Those are company positions, not findings by the Supreme Court.

The 2017 closure orders were later lifted. When the Supreme Court revived the Writ of Kalikasan case in
2022, it did not decide whether the companies caused the alleged damage; it ordered the Court of Appeals
to hear the case. Philippine EITI lists the four operations as active for fiscal year 2024. Eramen says it
uses contour mining and funds environmental and community programmes.
