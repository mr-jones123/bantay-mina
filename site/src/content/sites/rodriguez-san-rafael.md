## What the images show

In the 1988–1990 composite the hills east of Rodriguez town are forest and grass, crossed by the Marikina
River. There are no quarry pits. By 2026 two large pale quarry pits sit in the south-west of the box, west
of the green Pamitinan and Upper Marikina boundary line. In the 2016–2018 Sentinel-2 composite they were
only small patches, so most of their growth is recent.

The biggest change is on the east side, inside the protected-landscape boundary as BMB maps it: the Upper
Wawa Dam's reservoir (blue on the water-change layer) and the bare construction ground beside it. Of the
ground that went from green to bare or water inside the Upper Marikina boundary, about 136 ha (Landsat)
to 154 ha (Sentinel-2) became reservoir water. Most of the rest is dam construction ground. The magenta
in the north-west corner is the edge of Rodriguez town's housing growth, not quarrying.

The images show where land changed. They do not show the commercial quarries' permit boundaries, which
are not public, so the page does not say whether any pit crosses a protected-area line.

## On the Sierra Madre

No document reviewed for these San Rafael quarries places them in the range by name. The environmental
impact statement for ATN's quarry 7 km to the north, filed with the EMB, says that project "is part of the
Southern Sierra Madre". Its description of the same Montalban rock district names Montalban Millex among
the aggregate quarries operating there.

## Who operates here

MGB's September 2025 directory lists two commercial aggregate agreements in San Rafael. Montalban
Millex Aggregates Corporation holds MPSA No. 239-2007-IVA, covering 175.6893 hectares. San Rafael
Development Corporation holds MPSA No. 136-99-IV, covering 103.0972 hectares, with Majestic Earth Core
Ventures as authorized operator. These figures are authorized agreement areas, not measured areas of bare
or cleared ground. The public records reviewed do not establish the first year of physical quarrying at
either agreement.

The Upper Wawa Dam works farther east are a separate government bulk-water project, not a third
commercial MPSA. WawaJVCo's environmental summary lists Quarry 1 and Quarry 2 as temporary project
facilities in Barangay San Rafael, along with crushing and material-processing facilities used for dam
rockfill, filters and erosion protection. MWSS records say dam construction began on June 28, 2021 and
that foundation excavation and commissioning of the crushing plant were under way in 2022.

The project documents do not give coordinates for either temporary quarry. The bare construction ground
beside the reservoir is most likely part of the Upper Wawa works, but it has not been matched to Quarry 1
or Quarry 2, and it is not part of either commercial MPSA.

## What residents say

After Typhoon Ulysses in November 2020, Kasiglahan Village residents told GMA News that a failed dike
was partly responsible for flooding that destroyed homes. They also blamed mountain quarrying for the
flooding and thick mud. These were residents' allegations, not findings by the later DENR assessment.

## What local officials have said

Rodriguez Mayor Dennis Hernandez gave a different account: GMA reported that he attributed the severe
flooding to denuded mountains rather than quarrying. Rizal Governor Rebecca Ynares asked the national
government for a province-wide moratorium on land-development clearances, mining and quarrying. The
provincial environment officer told the Inquirer that saturated soil and Ulysses' excessive rainfall caused
the flood, and said the province's quarry companies operated outside protected areas. The latter was an
official's statement in a news report, not a published tenement-boundary analysis.

## What the government and companies say

MGB initially reported that quarry areas occupied 278 of the Marikina watershed's roughly 53,500
hectares and that no active quarry lay upstream of the old Wawa Dam during Ulysses. It said operators
reported their catch basins and settling ponds remained intact. DENR nevertheless suspended the San
Rafael Development and Montalban Millex agreements in December 2020 while a composite team investigated
the November 12 flood.

When MGB announced the lifting in February 2021, it said the composite team found quarrying's
contribution to Marikina River flooding and siltation to be minimal. The team identified the typhoon's
exceptional rainfall as the primary cause and cited prior watershed saturation, rapid urbanisation,
natural erosion, deforestation and other disturbance. Lifting was conditional on required drainage and
multiple silt traps, settling ponds or catchment basins, and desilting of affected tributaries.

## Protected areas nearby

Proclamation No. 901 set aside Pamitinan Protected Landscape in 1996; Proclamation No. 296 declared the
Upper Marikina River Basin Protected Landscape in 2011 and describes Pamitinan as part of its western
boundary. Republic Act No. 11038 later legislated them at 609.15 and 26,125.64 hectares, respectively,
and prohibits mineral exploration or extraction and commercial or large-scale quarrying within protected
areas while recognizing existing rights and government contracts subject to the Act.

The Upper Wawa project's own environmental summary states that the project site is within the Upper
Marikina River Basin Protected Landscape. It identifies the temporary project quarries in San Rafael, but
the accessible text does not map them. The dam works are governed by the project's own environmental and
protected-area approvals, not by a commercial mining agreement.

The public MGB records identify the two commercial agreements by barangay and area but do not provide
complete survey polygons, so their exact relationship to the protected-area boundaries cannot be read from
barangay names or point locations.

The 2022 DENR action near Masungi involved three different agreements: those of Rapid City Realty,
Quarry Rock Group and Quimzon Limestone. MGB said those contract areas were under care and maintenance,
had seen no mining since 2003, and showed no trace of mining or exploration during its May 26, 2022
reconnaissance. They are not the San Rafael quarries in these images.
