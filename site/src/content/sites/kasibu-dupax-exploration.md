## What the images show

This is a baseline, and it shows what it should: no mine. Between the 2024 and 2026 dry seasons the 4 km
box around Woggle's mapped Keon target has no new bare area that stands out. The magenta pixels are
scattered patches on farm plots and grassland, which change from year to year. Access tracks, drill pads
and plugged holes of the size the company and Dialogue Earth describe are narrower than the 10 m pixels.
The 2024 composite is also hazier than the 2026 one. If exploration turns into mining, this pair is the
record of what the land looked like before.


## Why the box is in Dupax

The Kasibu and Dupax disputes concern two different permits. MGB's August 2026 register lists NLMRC's
EP 000029-II over 4,456 hectares in Kasibu and Woggle's EP 000030-II over 3,101.11 hectares in Dupax
del Norte. They cannot be represented honestly by one small analysis box.

The Dupax site has the stronger location evidence. Metals Exploration published a WGS 84 / UTM Zone
51N map of the Keon target, planned drill collars, an access track and a drill pad. The mapped target is
in Barangay Bitnong, one of five permit barangays: Bitnong, Inaban, Munguia, Parai and Oyao. The
Kasibu permit covers Pao, Dine, Kakiduguen, Biyoy, Catarawan, Sigwen, Paquet and Muta, but no public
technical map or permit coordinates were found. Kasibu is therefore context here, not part of the image
box.

## What residents say

Residents of the five Dupax barangays say Woggle entered farms and cleared ground without landowner
consent, and that an information campaign was presented as consultation. The House resolution seeking
an inquiry records barangay and municipal resolutions making those allegations; they remain allegations,
not findings that the exploration was illegal. Residents kept the Bitnong barricade after the February
suspension because they sought cancellation rather than a pause.

In Kasibu, residents similarly dispute whether they gave informed consent to NLMRC. Bulatlat reports
that residents say attendance at a mushroom-growing activity was later used as proof of consent. NLMRC's
permit remained listed as valid and existing by MGB on 31 August 2026.

## What the government and company say

At a Senate hearing, the regional environment office said Woggle's permit area was 0.8% of the Magat
River forest reserve, did not drain directly into the Magat River and would have minimal impact provided
mitigation measures were included. Woggle's president said the company followed a 40-month approval
process and complied with government requirements. MGB nevertheless suspended the permit for public
safety and peace and order; the order was temporary, not a cancellation.

Exploration was not merely proposed. The company documented clearing for an access track and drill
pad, an induced-polarisation survey and limited drilling. It later said the target was deeper than expected
and no further work was planned in the short term. Dialogue Earth photographed a plugged drill hole and
workers removing pipe in March 2026. These are small features: a baseline comparison may show narrow
tracks, pads or plugged collars, but should not be expected to show the footprint of an operating mine.
