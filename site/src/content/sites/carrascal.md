## What the images show

The first "before" image predates the large mines. The Landsat archive has no scenes here before 1994,
and few after, so it combines the April–September views of 1994–2002. Eastern Mindanao has no real dry
season, and those are its clearest months. The hills behind the bay are almost all forest, with small
brown patches. There are no benches, roads or pits.

By 2026 an orange-brown mine complex, about 4 km long, spreads over the ridges south of the bay. This is
where the Carrascal Nickel Project's operating area is centred. Haul roads link it to the coast, and more
clearings run along ridge lines in the west and north. The magenta layer counts about 1,490 ha that went
from green to bare, roughly twice the mapped mine footprint inside the box. Much of the extra is newer
clearing that the 2023 footprint does not include.

The Sentinel-2 pair shows what changed after 2016–18, when the central complex was already open: new
clearing along the ridges to the north and west, an extension at the southern end, and bare ground at the
top edge of the box. That top edge is shared with the Claver page. The images show where land was
cleared. They do not show which permit each patch falls under.

## Who operates here

This is a cluster of surface nickel mines. CTP operates the Adlay project under MPSA No. 018-93-XI
(SMR) and the much smaller Dahican project under MPSA No. 158-2000-XIII (SMR). Carrascal Nickel
Corporation operates the Carrascal Nickel Project in Bon-ot under CTP's MPSA No. 243-2007-XIII
(SMR). Marcventures holds MPSA No. 016-93-XI across Carrascal and Cantilan; its Cabangahan, Pili and
Sipangpang mine areas lie south of the other projects.

The 14 km box is centred on the Carrascal Nickel tenure. A 2017 field study places the centre
of CNC's operational area at UTM Zone 51P 819376 E, 1036650 N — approximately 9.376° N, 125.907° E.
CTP's Adlay project also reaches the box, although the USGS's approximate project point at 9.36° N,
125.84° E is on its western edge. Marcventures' published MPSA corners run from 9°14′ to 9°20′ N, so
its three named mine areas sit just south of, or farther beyond, the box.

## What residents say

Residents' accounts have been consistent over time, but they are allegations rather than findings of
liability. Farmers and Cantilan officials told SunStar that floodwater in 2011 and 2012 carried thick
red sediment onto rice fields. Farmers said crops died, soil took several seasons to recover and silt
reduced irrigation capacity. Fishers on Ayoki Island said coastal water stayed brown longer after
mining began, their catch fell and they had to travel farther to fish.

In interviews conducted in 2025, farmers again described sticky sediment in rice fields and fishers
said nearshore water and the Ayoki marine sanctuary were covered by mud or silt. The Carac-an River
flows from the mining uplands through the plain and into Lanuza Bay, but that connection by itself does
not establish which operation caused any particular downstream deposit.

## What local officials have said

The Cantilan mayor told SunStar that about 80 percent of the town's 1,800 irrigated hectares was damaged
in the 2011 flood and that flooding recurred in 2012. In 2022, Carrascal officials asked MGB specialists
to examine discoloration and siltation in the Panikian River. The resulting government report linked
the condition to both upland mining and climatic factors and described effects on the Panikian Dam,
irrigation and farm productivity; it did not assign sole responsibility to one operator.

The general manager of the Cantilan Water District told Climate Rights International in 2025 that high
turbidity had forced the district to stop drawing from the Carac-an for a day in late 2024. He said the
river supplied almost 90 percent of Cantilan's water. His account describes the operational problem,
not a finding about its source.

## What the government and companies say

The government has treated both the watershed and mine impacts as regulatory concerns. In 2009 it
reserved 43,601 hectares around five waterways, including the Carac-an, Alamio and Panikian, as
critical watershed forest reserves while preserving pre-existing rights. DENR's 2016 audit led to
closure orders in 2017. Carrascal Nickel remained suspended after a 2018 review, subject to fines,
rehabilitation and corrective work, before DENR lifted the suspension in April 2019. The CTP Adlay
agreement was renewed that July. The 2025 MGB directory records the main projects as producing and
Dahican as under rehabilitation but active/producing.

A court-appointed commission reported in 2018 that mining had brought sediment, silt and heavy metals
to the Carac-an and Alamio rivers. When the residents' case against Marcventures was decided in August
2025, however, the trial court found that the plaintiffs had not proved that Marcventures — rather than
other legal and illegal mining and logging — caused the damage. The ruling therefore did not assign the
documented river impacts to that company.

Marcventures says its silt ponds, traps and booms keep surrounding waters clean. In response to Climate
Rights International, it said third parties test air and water quarterly, its own staff test monthly and
results are reported to EMB and MGB. It denied responsibility for the harms residents described. PCIJ
reported that CTP did not answer its requests for comment in 2022.
