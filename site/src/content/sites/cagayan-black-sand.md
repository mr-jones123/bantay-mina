## What the images show

Aparri town sits at the top left of the box, and the Cagayan River runs out to the sea along the left
side. In the 2020 Sentinel-2 composite a sandbar island sits in the river channel. By 2024 most of it has
gone, and the banks and bars along the channel have shifted. Switch on the water-change layer: blue
marks land that became water and lime marks water that became land. Removing sandbars was the stated aim
of the dredging, but river bars also move on their own, so the images show that the channel changed, not
why.

Dredging and magnetite separation on vessels cannot be seen from space. In this box the vegetation → bare
figure mostly reflects rice fields that were ploughed or harvested in one year and green in the other, not
mining. The Landsat pair around the 2012 Dodan raid shows no onshore pit large enough to pick out at 30 m.

## The place and its permits

This case joins two documented extraction episodes inside one low-lying Aparri corridor. In 2012 the NBI found an onshore magnetite operation in Barangay Dodan using heavy equipment. It said the operator had authority for ordinary sand and gravel but no mineral-processing permit. The later work was a government river-restoration project, not a magnetite-mining concession: Riverfront Construction and Great River North Consortium financed dredging and could dispose of the recovered sediment.

Riverfront's environmental study placed its 2.5-kilometre Segment 7 beside Barangay Toran, roughly six kilometres upstream of the river mouth. Toran's published barangay reference point is [18.3145° N, 121.6556° E](https://www.philatlas.com/luzon/r02/cagayan/aparri/toran.html). Dodan's reference point is [18.3362° N, 121.7036° E](https://www.philatlas.com/luzon/r02/cagayan/aparri/dodan.html). These points locate barangays, not permit boundaries or individual homes.

Pacific Offshore Exploration's separate 2019 operation illustrates why the legal categories matter. The provincial board sought its suspension after DPWH said it had issued no dredging permit. In a different Cagayan case, the Supreme Court held that a national sand-and-gravel permit and an environmental compliance certificate did not replace the required provincial permit. [That ruling](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2013/jul2013/gr_188500_2013.html) concerned a quarry in Peñablanca, not any Aparri operator, and is not a judgment on the projects shown here.

## Who lives here

The river mouth and adjacent Babuyan Channel are fishing grounds for Aparri communities. A DENR-supported research programme described the estuary as habitat for aramang or spider shrimp, ludong, hairtail, dolphinfish and croakers. It reported that aramang made up more than 39% of recorded catch in 1994–2004 and was a major local income and export product.

## What residents say

Fisherfolk told PCIJ that catch collapsed after dredging began, especially during the November 2021 aramang season. Municipal fee records reviewed by PCIJ showed the volume of dried aramang shipped from Aparri fell by more than 90% after the project began, although the data do not prove what caused the decline. Fishers also alleged that a dredger worked beyond the estuary in the Babuyan Channel and asked that the project be halted.

The project's own environmental study anticipated temporary resuspension of silt, higher turbidity, lower dissolved oxygen and less light penetration, with potential effects on aramang and other estuarine organisms. BFAR offered other possible explanations for the poor catch, including weather, overfishing, gear, more small boats and pandemic restrictions. Satellite images can test physical changes to bars, banks and dredging footprints, but cannot determine the cause of a fishery decline or prove that magnetite was separated from dredged sediment.

## What the government and companies say

The province and DENR presented the 2021 project as flood mitigation: remove obstructing sandbars, deepen the shallow river mouth and help reopen Aparri port. Riverfront said its small dredgers worked only in the river and transferred sediment offshore because the mother vessel was too large to enter. DENR's regional director said the agreement prohibited processing at the site and denied that the project was black-sand mining. The governor invited critics to inspect vessels for magnetic separators.

By October 2024, Aparri's mayor said the vessels were gone and fishing was beginning to recover. Malacañang said in April 2025 that the private operators had stopped in 2023, reportedly because dredged material had poor market conditions. House Resolution 1100 sought a new inquiry in June 2026; its claims remain allegations to be investigated, not findings of liability.
