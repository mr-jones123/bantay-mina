## What the images show

The first image predates the large mines. The Landsat archive has no scenes here before 1994, and few
after, so it combines every April–September view of 1994–2002. Those are the clearest months on Dinagat,
which has no real dry season. In that image the northern end of Dinagat is forest from coast to coast.
Small brown patches sit in the north and on the east coast, and a white blob of cloud that no view
escaped sits in the centre. Cloud in a "before" image can only lower the vegetation → bare count, never
raise it.

By 2024–26 a band of bare orange-brown ground crosses the ridge from the centre of the box to the east
coast, and widens into a large bare block above the east shore. More clearings mark the north end.
The magenta layer counts about 410 ha that went from green to bare, about 320 ha of it inside the mapped
mine footprint. The Landsat "after" uses three seasons, 2024–2026. With fewer, missed cloud in the south
of the box showed up as new bare ground. A small patch of cloud remains at the bottom edge.

The Sentinel-2 pair shows the band still spreading after 2016–18, mostly along its northern arm and down
the east coast. Several agreement areas share this ridge, and the page does not assign individual
patches to one of them.

## Who operates here

This box covers the ridge above Panamaon and Esperanza in Loreto. The published technical boundary
for Sinosteel's 972-hectare H.Y. project runs from 10°24′30″ to 10°25′30″ north and 125°37′00″ to
125°40′00″ east, wholly inside the box. Panamaon's mapped settlement coordinate, 10.4185° N,
125.6129° E, is also inside its western edge. Century Peak's 1,198-hectare Casiguran project and
Oriental Synergy's 648-hectare Bel-at project occupy the same Panamaon–Esperanza mining corridor.
Comet's 1,296-hectare agreement reaches the box's western side. Krominco's Redondo chromite area
straddles the southern edge, so this view does not capture that entire 757-hectare agreement.

The permits are not interchangeable. Sinosteel holds MPSA No. 002-90-X; Comet holds No. 003-90-X
and names El Terrafirma as operator; Century Peak holds No. 010-92-X; Oriental Synergy holds
No. 011-92-X; and Krominco holds No. 291-2009-XIII. MGB's September 2025 directory records different
operating states across the cluster rather than one status for the whole ridge.

## Why the mineral reservation matters

Proclamation No. 391 placed Dinagat's mineral lands in the Surigao Mineral Reservation in 1939. It
reserved the minerals to the state, put the area under the Bureau of Mines and closed it to ordinary
mining location and lease. It did not by itself grant any of today's companies a right to mine. Those
rights come from the later, numbered production-sharing agreements and their environmental and
operating approvals.

The reservation also complicates land tenure. A mine agreement gives access to state-owned minerals,
not ownership of every farm or home above them. Later proclamations excluded some Dinagat parcels
from the reservation, but the MGB records for all five projects here continue to label their agreements
“SMR,” for Surigao Mineral Reservation.

## What residents say

In November 2023, associations of irrigators and farmers from Esperanza and Panamaon brought a
complaint to PH-EITI about siltation of farmland and mangroves. They sought rehabilitation,
compensation and identification of the responsible operation or operations. The public case summary
does not attribute the sediment to a particular company. PH-EITI's multi-stakeholder group instead
recommended a science-based investigation to trace the material and its source.

## What the government and companies say

The regulatory record changes over time. The 2016 audit led to adverse findings and closure or
suspension actions against several Loreto operations. Oriental Synergy's cancellation was affirmed in
2018, while Krominco's order became a suspension with corrective conditions. Oriental Synergy later
received an MPSA renewal in 2024, but MGB still listed it under care and maintenance in September
2025. Krominco had the same care-and-maintenance status. Sinosteel was in commercial operation;
Century Peak was listed as producing, although MGB's production table recorded no output for its
project in 2024; and Comet was non-producing.

PCIJ asked Oriental Synergy to respond to the audit findings in 2022 and reported receiving no reply.
Century Peak's own project history says it acquired the Casiguran agreement in 2006. That company
history and the current MGB directory identify the renewed agreement as valid; that status does not
resolve the separate 2023 community complaint about where the silt came from.
