## What the images show

Itogon has been mined since 1906, so no image here is "before mining". Baguio's airport and the edge of
the city are at the left of the box. In the 1988–1990 composite the hills are browner and cut by many pale
scars along ridges and creeks. By 2026 much more of the land is green. Most of the mining here is
underground: industrial tunnels at Acupan and Sangilo, and small-scale workings whose portals and pits are
far smaller than a 10–30 m pixel. The images therefore show surface scars, spoil and sediment, not the
mining itself.

The clearest new bare areas are a pale strip and a few patches in the north of the box that appear by
2026, and one patch in the south in the Sentinel-2 pair. This site does not say who made them. The Ucab
debris flow covered about two hectares, too small to pick out reliably at this scale.

## A district shaped by mining

Itogon's municipal history records gold gathering and trade before industrial mining. Benguet Corporation
started at Antamok in 1906 and Acupan in 1927. When Acupan closed in 1992, the loss of mine jobs helped
push more residents into small-scale mining. The municipality says small-scale mining now takes place in
nearly all of Itogon's barangays.

Industrial mining also continues. MGB-CAR's 2023 directory lists Benguet Corporation's Acupan Contract
Mining Project at Balatoc and Itogon-Suyoc Resources' Sangilo mine as producing patented-claim operations.
The Sangilo project's environmental filing places its claims and facilities in Poblacion and Ampucao, southeast
of Acupan-Balatoc.

## The Ucab disaster

On 15 September 2018, extreme rain from Typhoon Ompong triggered a debris flow at First Gate in Barangay
Ucab. PAGASA's official review says it crossed about two hectares, buried bunkhouses and shanties, and killed
51 people. A concrete bunkhouse that had become a chapel and storm shelter was among the structures buried.
PAGASA says the whole site was mapped as very highly susceptible to rain-induced landslides and unsuitable
for habitation.

## What the government and company say

Two days after the disaster, the DENR secretary ordered all small-scale mining in the Cordillera closed.
MGB-CAR later rated assessed sites at First Gate, other parts of Ucab, and mining areas in Loacan, Poblacion,
Virac and Tuding as very highly susceptible to landslides.

Benguet Corporation said the Ucab miners were working without its permission inside its Antamok claims. It
said it had suspended operations there in the late 1990s, warned occupants that the area was a geohazard, and
asked them to leave. Those are the company's claims, reported by GMA News; they do not establish what caused
the debris flow. PAGASA attributed the disaster to extreme rainfall, unstable terrain and exposure within a
mapped no-build zone.
