## What the images show

There is no "before quarrying" image here. Quarrying in this district predates the Landsat record. In the
1988–1990 composite a pale quarry already runs north–south in the lower middle of the box, in the
Minuyan–Bigte area. The rest is forest, grass and farms on both sides of the Angat River, which winds
across the middle.

By 2026 there are two large white pits with blue-green pit lakes. The southern one, where Republic
Cement's Minuyan project is, has grown well beyond the 1988 quarry. The northern one, in Matictic where
Helix's Quarry 3 lies, was not there in 1988–90. It was already open in 2016–2018 and has widened since.
Smaller pale clearings dot the hills around both.

The magenta layer counts about 218 ha that went from green to bare between 1988–90 and 2026, and about
274 ha between 2016–18 and 2026. The second figure includes many small farm plots and some new housing
in the south-west of the box, not only quarry ground. The images do not show which permit each pit or
clearing belongs to.

## On the Sierra Madre

A 2021 geological assessment of Pinagrealan Cave in Bigte, Norzagaray, just south of this box, written
for a petition against a nearby quarry, describes the cave as "located at the foot of the Sierra Madre
mountain range" and the area's Angat limestone as lying "on the western flank of the southern Sierra
Madre". It is a private report, not a government finding.

## Who operates here

This box covers parts of a long-established cement and quarry district rather than one mine. Republic Cement's
Minuyan project is the southern operation. Its 2020 ECC covers quarrying and cement manufacturing within a
559-hectare project area and allows quarry extraction of up to 3.5 million tonnes a year. Those 559 hectares are
an authorized project area, not the area actually cleared. Republic's 2019 project summary places the existing
Bulacan plant at about 14.8675° N, 121.0875° E. A separate 2002 ECC publishes the corners of a 9.6-hectare
Minuyan quarry block at roughly 14.8700–14.8739° N and 121.0884–121.0910° E, inside this box.

Helix Mining and Development Corporation, formerly Holcim Mining and Development Corporation, operates the
northern limestone-and-shale complex. Its 2019 ECC links Quarry 1 and Quarry 2 to MPSA No. 027-94-III and the
342.6522-hectare Quarry 3 to MPSA No. 140-99-III. The certificate describes 390 hectares of quarry and related
areas across Matictic and Bangkal in Norzagaray and Bayabas in Doña Remedios Trinidad. Again, those are
permitted project and pit areas, not a measure of bare ground.

MGB also lists Spar Development's 24-hectare MPSA No. 201-2004-III in Bigte as a commercial operation. The
public MGB directory reviewed here gives only the barangay, not a survey-corner schedule. The Bigte barangay
centre and the mapped Rhino Rock associated with the permit are south of this box, so Spar should be treated as
a nearby operation rather than assigned to a clearing in the box without the official MPSA polygon.

## What residents say

A 2009 University of the Philippines study surveyed host communities in Matictic, Minuyan and Bigte. Residents
in Matictic and Minuyan ranked quarry-related road damage, vehicle damage and accidents as their leading
problem; Bigte residents ranked ground vibration from blasting first. Bigte respondents also identified dust from
blasting, although the study says it was the least-selected problem there. These are residents' assessments, not
regulatory findings that attribute each impact to a particular permit holder.

At Republic Cement's September 2019 hearing in Minuyan, one resident said dust from the plant reached the
community during the dry, windy season and asked that the road be watered. A barangay official raised exhaust
from cement trucks parked along the access road, while another resident reported ash-like dust on vegetables.
Republic said it used road watering and would investigate; its consultant said modelling did not support the
plant as the source at one resident's location and suggested road traffic or another nearby plant. The hearing
record does not establish the source of all reported dust.

## What local officials have said

At the same hearing, then mayor Alfredo Germar called himself pro-development but asked Republic Cement to
ensure its contractors obtained business permits and paid the correct taxes. He listed health, flooding, water,
waste and canal improvement among Norzagaray's municipal needs and asked the barangay and company for help.
The record does not say the quarry caused those problems.

In 2015, a Norzagaray councillor told Punto! Central Luzon that the Rhino and Lioness rock formations were
within Spar's 24-hectare mining site. He said the municipal council planned to ask DENR to exclude the formations
from the MPSA and preserve them as a tourism site. The report said Punto! was still seeking Spar's response.

## Protected areas nearby

Proclamation No. 505 says that Proclamation No. 71 reserved a parcel spanning several municipalities, including
Norzagaray, for watershed purposes and transferred its administration to the National Power Corporation. That
municipality-level wording does not establish that any of these quarry polygons is inside the Angat Watershed
Reservation. None of the quarry ECCs reviewed here states that its project is inside the Angat or Ipo watershed,
so an overlap should not be claimed without the official watershed and MPSA boundaries.

The more specific cave record is Republic Cement's 2002 ECC. It excluded 12 hectares that DENR had recommended
as part of the **proposed** Pinagrealan Cave Protected Landscape from the 21.6-hectare quarry proposal. The
certificate does not say that a protected landscape was later proclaimed. Separately, Republic Act No. 9072
protects caves and cave resources nationwide and makes DENR the lead implementing agency; that law does not by
itself draw a Pinagrealan protected-landscape boundary.

## What the government and companies say

The September 2018 stoppage was temporary and regional, not a finding that the Norzagaray operators had broken
the law. DENR suspended quarrying in eight regions on September 21 after the fatal Naga, Cebu landslide. On
September 27, after MGB review, it named Holcim Mining and Development Corporation and Republic Cement among
the Region III companies allowed to resume. The published list did not name Spar, so its exact lifting date is
not established by that report.

The 2019 Helix/Holcim ECC covers existing quarry operations and superseded a 2013 certificate. It sets a combined
maximum extraction rate of 6.4 million tonnes a year: 6.06 million tonnes of limestone under MPSA No. 140-99-III
and 340,000 tonnes of shale under No. 027-94-III. It requires stockpiles and silt from settling ponds to be kept
away from water bodies and drainage systems, and says quarrying must remain within approved MPSA areas.

Republic Cement's 2020 ECC superseded its July 1996 certificate and a May 2011 amendment. It requires buffer
zones around the quarry and plant and near water bodies. Republic's project summary says process water comes
from a local supplier, a groundwater well, rain and recycled water, and is used in part for dust suppression.
These are permit conditions and company descriptions, not proof that impacts never occur.

Both operators sought further changes in 2025. EMB notices scheduled public scoping for Helix's proposed
production increase and quarry-area expansion in Matictic, Baraka and Bangkal, and for Republic Cement's
proposed Minuyan quarry expansion and cement-manufacturing project. Public scoping gathers issues for the EIA;
neither notice is an ECC or approval to expand.
