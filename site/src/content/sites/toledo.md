## What the images show

Copper has been mined here since 1955, so there is no "before mining" image. The first pair begins in
1992–95, when the pits were already open. It uses March–August scenes, the clearest months over the Cebu
uplands. The dark lake at the top of the box is Malubog reservoir. The mine runs across the middle of the
box from south-west to north-east: Lutopan in the south-west, Carmen in the centre, and the former Biga pit
(now the tailings storage facility) in the east.

In 1992–95 the mine was one long, pale band of benches and dumps. By 2026 the pits are deeper, ringed and
partly flooded with green and turquoise water, and much of the old dump ground around them has grown back
green. The new clearing (magenta) is at the edges: the north and west rims of the complex, and around the
Biga facility.

The Sentinel-2 pair shows where most change since 2016–18 has happened: a band of magenta around the Biga
tailings facility, whose footprint has spread outward on every side. A thin magenta line to the south-east
follows a cleared corridor. These images do not show what that corridor carries.

## Who operates here

Atlas Consolidated Mining and Development Corporation holds the underlying mineral rights. Its wholly
owned subsidiary, Carmen Copper Corporation, operates the Toledo copper mine under a 2006 operating
agreement. The complex contains the Lutopan, Biga and Carmen copper deposits. Carmen is the only orebody
that Atlas says is actively mined today: Lutopan stopped producing in 2015, while the former Biga open pit
is now the Biga Tailings Storage Facility.

MGB's 2025 profile lists MPSA Nos. 210-2005-VII and 264-2008-VII as producing and No. 307-2009-VII as
not producing. The current tailings facility is at Biga. Sangi is the concentrate-shipping terminal, not a
tailings dam, and Atlas's 2025 filing identifies Malubog Dam as a company reservoir that now also hosts a
floating solar facility.

## What residents say

In 2019, officials and residents of Biga asked the city mayor and MGB to intervene. They alleged that mine
blasting had cracked walls and floors, that a community spring had dried up, and that mine-waste piles and
blasting posed risks to health, homes and safety. These were community allegations, not findings reported
by the Inquirer; the newspaper said it could not reach Carmen Copper for comment.

## What local officials have said

After MGB found the emergency tailings pond leaking in 2008, the Cebu governor directed Carmen Copper to
monitor its main pipe. A DENR inspection found milky water and deposits along the Sapangdaku River, while
the MGB regional director said the sampled water's pH met DENR standards and was not toxic.

## What the government and companies say

The 1999 incident and the 2008 leak are separate. Executive Order No. 96 records that mine water from
Atlas's then-abandoned Biga open pit spilled into the Sapangdaku River and Tañon Strait in August 1999;
it does not call that release a tailings-dam failure. In September 2008, MGB found slurry from Carmen
Copper's emergency tailings pond draining toward Sigpit Creek. DENR stopped use of that pond while an
investigation examined the cause and impacts. Carmen Copper's resident manager said the material was not
toxic; DENR nevertheless cited failures to disclose the facility in the environmental programme submitted
to MGB.

Carmen Copper was one of 12 companies that DENR said passed the nationwide mine audit announced in
February 2017, so it was not among the mines closed or suspended in that audit. A separate safety suspension
followed the December 2020 Carmen Pit landslide. The company attributed the slide to prolonged rain
aggravated by Typhoon Vicky; MGB suspended pit operations to prevent further accidents. DENR allowed a
partial restart in March 2021 but kept the affected areas excluded and imposed safety conditions. In May
2024 the company again stopped work in the pit's deepest section on safety grounds while shifting to other
stages of its redevelopment plan. MGB still classified its two main MPSAs as producing in 2025.
