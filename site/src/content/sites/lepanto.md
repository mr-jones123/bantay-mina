## What the images show

There is no "before mining" image here either. Lepanto had been mining for more than 50 years by 1988.
Most of the mine is underground, so the surface shows much less than it does at open-pit sites. What is
visible is a chain of ponds and pale deposits along the valley floor in the north of the box, with mine
buildings and settlements scattered down the valley. Between 1988–90 and 2026, the pond at the northern end
became a large blue-green impoundment. The vegetation → bare figures here are small, because the land that
was already cleared by 1988 does not count as new loss.

## Who lives here

People from Mankayan identify as Kankanaey. A PH-EITI study based on fieldwork in 2019 describes farming
and small-scale mining as continuing livelihoods. Women interviewed in Paco, Suyoc and Taneg reported
shrinking water supplies and sinking ground. Some attributed the subsidence to Lepanto's underground
workings. The study records those accounts but does not establish that mining was the sole cause.

## What residents say

In January 2012, residents of Madaymen in Barangay Tabio blocked drilling for the Far Southeast project.
They told Northern Dispatch that they had not been consulted and had not allowed mining in their ancestral
land. An NCIP hearing officer first ordered drilling to stop, then reversed that order after the company
sought reconsideration. The barricade remained until the contractor removed its rigs in January 2013.

## Tailings and the Abra River

Bulatlat reported in 2008 that EMB-DENR was checking Tailings Dam 5A after an April sample exceeded the
lead limit. The report also summarized university studies that found acidic discharge and elevated cyanide
and metals at some points from the mine area into the Abra River system. Lepanto rejected those studies as
opposition propaganda. The company now says Dam 5A effluent is monitored daily and that quarterly samples
collected by a multipartite team have met DENR standards.

## What the government and company say

The Supreme Court ruled in 2022 that the MPSA cannot be renewed without the consent of the Mankayan
Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples. Lepanto says an FPIC agreement was signed in July 2026,
but still required NCIP approval and a Certification Precondition before MGB could act on renewal. The
company also reported that Victoria and Teresa remained in production in the first half of 2026.
