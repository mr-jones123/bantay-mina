## What the images show

The first image is not "before mining". Taganito had been mined since 1987. The Landsat archive has no
scenes here before 1994, and few after, so the image combines the April–September views of 1994–2002.
Eastern Mindanao has no real dry season, and those are its clearest months. In 1994–2002 the coastal
ridges show brown and orange patches in the north-west, where the Taganito mine was. The rest of the
box is forest.

By 2026 bare orange-brown laterite runs along almost every ridge in the northern half of the box, and a
belt of clearing crosses it from west to east. The processing plant and a large red-brown impoundment sit
near the coast in Hayanggabon, where Taganito HPAL was built in 2010–2013. The magenta layer counts about
2,300 ha that went from green to bare, and about 1,300 ha of that is inside the mapped mine footprint.

The Sentinel-2 pair shows the spread after 2016–18: new clearing on the western ridges, along the southern
edge of the belt, and in long arms reaching south into the forest in the east and south-east. The box
stops at 9.44° N. The mines farther south are on the Carrascal page, and the two boxes overlap by about
1.5 km.

## Who operates here

This is a mining and processing district rather than one project. Taganito Mining Corporation (TMC), a
Nickel Asia subsidiary, mines near-surface laterite across Hayanggabon, Urbiztondo, Taganito and Cagdianao
under MPSA No. 266-2008-XIII. It exports saprolite and limonite and supplies low-grade limonite to the
adjacent Taganito HPAL plant, which makes a nickel-cobalt intermediate for refining in Japan.

Farther east, Platinum Group Metals Corporation (PGMC), part of Global Ferronickel Holdings, operates the
Cagdianao mine. Surigao Integrated Resources Corporation holds MPSA No. 007-92-X; the agreement was renewed
and enlarged to 5,219.56 hectares in 2016. The company reports seasonal mining and shipping from April to
October and began its 2026 season in April.

The wider district also includes Adnama's Urbiztondo project. PCIJ recorded nickel production there in 2020,
but also reported that Adnama was among the mines ordered closed after the 2016 audit and that its operating
status was unresolved in the records PCIJ reviewed. Claver Mineral Development Corporation's much smaller
Cagdianao operation, formerly operated by Shenzhou Mining Group, was ordered permanently closed in 2018.

## The Mamanwa ancestral domain

The overlap is documented, not inferred from proximity alone. In 2011, MGB officials told the Philippine
Daily Inquirer that the TMC sites in Taganito and Urbiztondo and PGMC's Cagdianao site were within a
48,870-hectare Certificate of Ancestral Domain Title held by 17 Mamanwa tribes. TMC now also describes its
operating area as part of the Mamanwa ancestral domain.

The Supreme Court record identifies the title as CADT No. R13-CLA-0906-048, spanning parts of Claver and four
other municipalities. Its 2022 decision concerned Shenzhou, not every mine in the district. The Court upheld
an NCIP order voiding Shenzhou's compliance certificate because its signatory lacked authority, and ordered
Shenzhou to stop work in the covered area without proper free, prior and informed consent, return possession
and pay agreed royalties.

## What residents and independent monitors say

Friends of the Earth Japan tested river and domestic water around Taganito and a Mamanwa resettlement site in
2012 and 2013. It reported hexavalent chromium in the Taganito and Hayanggabon rivers above the Japanese
standard it used, and at the Japanese and WHO reference level in water available at the resettlement site.
Residents interviewed by the group said some people still drank that water. These were civil-society tests,
not a finding by a Philippine court or regulator that a particular company caused the readings.

In the dry season of 2019, the same group did not find hexavalent chromium above its Japanese reference level
in the two rivers. It did report nickel above a Japanese water-supply control target and hexavalent chromium
at the reference level in water PGMC supplied near another resettlement site. Residents told the group they
had been warned not to drink it but used it for cooking, bathing and washing.

## What the government and companies say

The official record is mixed. In 2011, MGB officials said TMC, THPAL and PGMC had been cited and fined over
Claver Bay pollution but had taken mitigating measures; they said the issues then did not justify suspension.
In 2012, the MGB suspended Shenzhou after a government-led team found extensive coastal siltation and said its
settling pond had worsened the problem. The five neighbouring companies that were also warned—including TMC,
THPAL, PGMC and Adnama—reported control measures and were given 60 days to complete them.

The 2016 audit produced closure action against Adnama and permanent closure of CMDC. Adnama later told PCIJ
that its area was not in a proclaimed or critical watershed where mining was prohibited, that regulators
inspected it quarterly, and that it had implemented monitoring-team recommendations. TMC says it protects the
Taganito River and runs environmental and community programmes. PGMC describes Cagdianao as a surface mine
worked with trucks and excavators. These are company accounts.

A separate 2025 Supreme Court case dealt with money promised to Mamanwa communities, not mine pollution. The
Court upheld audit disallowances after NCIP officials spent part of THPAL's annual assistance on regional-office
costs rather than the socioeconomic projects specified in the 2009 agreement. The ruling distinguished that
assistance from TMC's one-percent royalty obligation to Mamanwa communities.
