## What the images show

Nonoc Island is at the top left of the box and Hinatuan Island at the bottom right. Neither "before"
image predates mining. Nonoc had been mined from 1974 to 1986, and Tagana-an on Hinatuan since 1980.
The Landsat archive also has no scenes here before 1994. Few scenes exist after that either, so the first
"before" combines the April–September views of nine years, 1994–2002.

By then Nonoc already had wide orange-brown patches of bare laterite across its southern half. By 2026
some of that old ground has turned green again. New clearing (magenta) has opened along the island's
eastern peninsula and in patches in the south. Hinatuan Island changed more. In 1994–2002 bare ground
covered part of its southern end. By 2026 most of the island's interior is bare, and the magenta layer
marks the largest block of green-to-bare change in the box.

The Sentinel-2 pair shows mining on Hinatuan still widening after 2016–18, and fresh clearing along
Nonoc's south coast. The page does not say which company cleared a given patch on Nonoc. Contractors
worked parts of the island in the 2000s and early 2010s.

## Who operates here

The view joins two separate nickel districts. The western cluster is on Nonoc Island in Surigao City.
Pacific Nickel holds MPSA No. 072-97-XIII-SMR-Amended A. It includes the remains of the mine and
integrated refinery formerly operated by Marinduque Mining and later held by Nonoc Mining and Industrial
Corporation. Contractors extracted and directly shipped ore from parts of Nonoc in the 2000s and early
2010s, but San Miguel's March 2026 accounts say the wider Nonoc project has not started commercial
operations under the present Pacific Nickel–Philnico structure.

The eastern cluster is HMC's Tagana-an mine on Hinatuan Island, Barangay Talavera. It is not part of the
Nonoc project. HMC, wholly owned by Nickel Asia, has mined there since 1980 and holds a separate
773.77-hectare agreement. MGB records it as active and producing; Nickel Asia says it uses surface mining
and that the revised mine plan extends through 2026.

## What residents say

Accounts from Nonoc show how closely livelihoods became tied to mining. Residents interviewed by the
Inquirer in 2012 remembered the 1975–1982 period as a time of plentiful mine work and local businesses.
After shutdown, former workers and fishers described scarce employment and difficult fishing. Hundreds
of residents and mine workers rallied in Surigao City in October 2012 asking the national government to
resolve the legal disputes and allow work to resume.

The same report attributed weak fish catches to overfishing, destructive fishing methods and bad weather;
it did not establish that the Nonoc mine caused the decline. The retrieved reporting therefore does not
support turning those livelihood accounts into a claim of mine-caused damage.

## What the government and companies say

The 2016 government audit found HMC out of compliance with two conditions of its environmental
compliance certificate and called for better controls on mud and loading spills, coastal sediment collectors,
rehabilitation and an independent study of the island's carrying capacity. The DENR's February 2017 order
said mining had impaired watershed functions and the integrity of Hinatuan Island. Nickel Asia rejected the
legal and technical basis for cancelling the agreement and said it would pursue all remedies. Later MGB
registers list the Tagana-an agreement as active and producing.

HMC told the Philippine Star in 2017 that it had rehabilitated 79 hectares and was restoring another 59
hectares by backfilling, stabilising slopes and planting trees and crops. Those are company statements. The
2014 technical report commissioned for Pacific Nickel separately described Nonoc's mined parcels and
support areas as disturbed, much of them bare, while noting rehabilitation in some mined-out areas.

Nonoc's permit remains in force, but a permit is not proof of current production. MGB's July 2024 register
places 1,378 hectares of Pacific Nickel's retained agreement under approved feasibility declarations and the
rest in exploration. San Miguel's filing says commercial operations had still not begun by March 2026. The
Department of Finance has described the old government-held Nonoc assets as idle and tied up in decades
of litigation over their sale and unpaid purchase price.
